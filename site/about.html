About this data

This is a selection, not everything. It is what the District's Contracts and Procurement Transparency Portal listed when we pulled it, weekly since August 2026. Records its search had stopped returning before then are not here, and a record it never published cannot be. Payments often carry no contract number, or one not on DC's published contract list, so the contracts here cover only part of what was paid. None of it can be checked against the District's own accounts; it is what DC's site shows.

Fields are shown as the District entered them. Nothing comes from a model and nothing is estimated. A few things are added by hand, each from a file that cites its source: agency names published only as an abbreviation or a budget code (listed below), amounts the District's own paperwork contradicts (below), how some contracts were awarded (the "of interest" badges), and, on the renewals page, how many options a few contracts allow. The technology page groups DC's commodity codes and lists every code it uses.

About the source: contracts.ocp.dc.gov

The Contracts and Procurement Transparency Portal (contracts.ocp.dc.gov) is the District's public site for its contracting records. In its own words, it "was created by the Office of Contracting and Procurement (OCP) … as a public clearinghouse for all information related to the District Government's contracting and procurement efforts." Anyone can search it. This site copies four of its sections so they can be filtered and totalled, and keeps contracts, orders and payments after DC's site drops them.

The CPO is the Chief Procurement Officer, who heads OCP. Agencies on that list, such as the Office of the Chief Financial Officer and the Attorney General, contract outside OCP's authority, so the site's other sections may hold only part of their contracting, or none of it.

Things to know before you total anything

Amounts: what is shown, and why

DC's site gives each option period its own amount. We checked those amounts against the District's own paperwork posted beside them: the signed contract, the modification exercising the option, and the price determination. On 12 October 2026 we drew 60 option periods at random and found paperwork stating the period's amount for 48. DC's figure disagreed with it for 14 of those 48, about three in ten (with a sample this size, roughly 18% to 43%). Most often the option period just repeats the base year's amount. The same check on 60 base periods found 5 of 48 that disagreed, about one in ten (roughly 5% to 22%).

So this site shows and totals base-period amounts only. Option periods are still listed, with their dates, labels and documents, but not their amounts. Base-period amounts are DC's entries and about one in ten may be wrong; read the signed award before relying on one. Each sampled period and the document it was checked against are in amount_checks.yaml.

Records corrected by hand

Where the District's own paperwork contradicts DC's figure, the paperwork's figure is used. DC's is kept beside it as amount and amount_text. Each correction names its document in corrections.yaml.

One award listed under more than one number

Most are DDOT contracts listed once under the number on the contract itself (with the payments) and again under a CW number (with the documents). Totals count one record per award.

Agency names filled in

Every table keeps the published value beside the name shown, as agency_as_published. Values with no source, such as ELC, are left as published. Abbreviations are never merged on their own: two different agencies publish as OPC.

What each column is

Columns ending in _text are exactly as published; the numbers and dates beside them are parsed from those.
