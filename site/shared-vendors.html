Vendors, by how many agencies they sell to

One row per vendor name exactly as spelled on DC's site (why names aren't merged). Click ▸ for each agency's contract titles and orders; click a heading to sort, and hover it for what it counts. One vendor at many agencies doesn't mean one product. Some of the vendors with the most agencies are resellers of many makers' products (Carahsoft's contract titles name Salesforce, Dell, Splunk and Microsoft, among others). Read a row's titles before treating its spread as one purchase repeated.

How the product search works

Each agency types its own titles, so a product may be spelled several ways (try a shorter word), and a word can match a title about something else. Purchase orders have no title on DC's site and can't be searched by product; the second table matches the text against the supplier name on orders instead, which answers a different question.

Why vendor names aren't merged here

DC's site doesn't standardise vendor names. One company can be "CDW GOVERNMENT LLC" on a contract and "CDW GOVERNMENT INC" on an order; each spelling is its own row above. A contract's vendor and an order's supplier are joined only when spelled exactly alike, capitals included. Supplier names on orders stop at 40 characters ("Mary's Center for Maternal and Child Car"), so a long name on an order won't match its contract spelling.

This repository has a file of vendor-name merges, vendor_aliases.yaml, that isn't used here. Its merges come from a mechanical rule (strip punctuation and endings such as INC, LLC and CORP, then group names that come out the same), are recorded only for one set of technology payments, and cite no source. (Each agency-name fix in agency_names.yaml cites the source that ties the two names together.) A rule like that can join two different companies. To group spellings yourself, type part of a name in "Name contains": the box under it totals every matching spelling, counting each agency, contract and order once.
