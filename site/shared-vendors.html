This page only counts what DC's site lists; it can't tell you whether a single purchase would have been cheaper or possible.

The same product at several agencies

Type a product or brand name. You get every contract whose title contains it, grouped by agency. Titles are typed by each agency, so the same product may be spelled differently (try a shorter word), and a word can match a title that isn't about that product: read the titles before counting anything. Purchase orders have no title on DC's site, so they can't be searched by product; the second table matches the text against the supplier name on orders instead, which is a different question.

Why vendor names aren't merged here

DC's site doesn't standardise vendor names. The same company can be "CDW GOVERNMENT LLC" on a contract and "CDW GOVERNMENT INC" on an order, and each spelling is its own row above. Contracts and orders also spell names differently from each other: a contract's vendor and an order's supplier are joined only when the two are spelled exactly alike, capitals included. Supplier names on orders stop at 40 characters ("Mary's Center for Maternal and Child Car"), so a long name on an order won't match its contract spelling.

This repository has a file of vendor-name merges, vendor_aliases.yaml. It isn't used here. Its merges come from a mechanical rule (strip punctuation and endings such as INC, LLC and CORP, then group names that come out the same), and the file records them only for one set of technology payments. Unlike the agency-name fixes in agency_names.yaml, where each entry cites the source that ties the two names together, no merge in it cites a source. A rule like that can also join two different companies. So this page shows names as spelled. To group spellings yourself, type part of the name in "Name contains": the box under it then gives totals for every matching spelling together, counting each agency, contract and order once.

One vendor at many agencies doesn't mean one product. Some of the vendors with the most agencies are resellers that sell many makers' products. Carahsoft's contract titles, for example, name Salesforce, Dell, Splunk and Microsoft. Expand a row and read the titles before treating a vendor's spread as a single purchase repeated.
