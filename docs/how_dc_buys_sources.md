# Sources for site/how-dc-buys.html

Every factual claim on the page, the primary source it rests on, and the words quoted from that source.
All sources were fetched and read on **12 October 2026**.

- D.C. Code text is from the Council's official site, code.dccouncil.gov (`https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/<section>`).
  The page does not print a "current through" date. Where a section carries temporary legislation, that is noted.
- DCMR text is from dcregs.dc.gov. Each section page links a Word file of the "most recent version of the final adopted rule";
  the quotes below are from that file. The section page URL is
  `https://www.dcregs.dc.gov/Common/DCMR/SectionList.aspx?SectionNumber=<title>-<section>`. Effective dates are as the section page shows them.
- Quotes are verbatim, including the sources' curly apostrophes. "[...]" marks an omission.

Not used because I could not confirm them from a primary source: any Mayor's Order (none found that sets IT or procurement approval
rules beyond what is below), and any OCP or OCTO dollar threshold other than those quoted here.

## Council approval

| Claim | Source | Quoted text |
|---|---|---|
| Contracts over $1 million in a 12-month period need Council approval. | [D.C. | Deemed approval: 10 days with no resolution, or 45 days without disapproval. (The page says "within 10 days of the Council receiving it"; the statute counts from the first day, excluding weekends and holidays, after receipt. The page does not say "business days", because the parenthetical attaches to the start day, not the whole period.) | § 1-204.51(b)(2); [§ 2-352.02(b)(2)](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/2-352.02) | "(A) During the 10-day period beginning on the 1st day (excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and holidays) following its receipt by the Office of the Secretary to the Council, no member of the Council introduces a resolution to approve or disapprove the proposed contract; or (B) If a resolution has been introduced [...], and the Council does not disapprove the contract during the 45-day review period" |
| Multiyear contracts need affirmative Council approval; silence means disapproval. | § 1-204.51(c)(3); § 2-352.02(b)(1) | "The Council shall be required to take affirmative action to approve the contract within 45 days. If no action is taken to approve the contract within 45 calendar days, the contract shall be deemed disapproved." |
| Contracts over 5 years (from multi-year money) need a two-thirds Council vote. | § 1-204.51(a) | "No contract involving expenditures out of an appropriation which is available for more than 1 year shall be made for a period of more than 5 years unless, with respect to a particular contract, the Council, by a two-thirds vote of its members present and voting, authorizes the extension of such period for such contract." |
| Exercising an option on a $1M+ contract needs its own Council submission. | § 2-352.02(a)(2), (b)(3)(B) | "Council approval of a contract containing a provision that grants to the District the option of continuing or amending the contract beyond the 12-month period of Council approval shall not constitute Council approval of the exercise of the option contract." / "The exercise of an option that meets the criteria for Council review under this subsection without Council review of the option contract is a violation of this section and § 1-204.51." |

contractor (2499, FFP definition); cost-reimbursement types pay the contractor's allowable costs up to a ceiling, so cost growth under
the ceiling is paid by the District (2499, cost-reimbursement definition); T&M and labor-hour pay fixed hourly rates for however many
hours are worked, up to a ceiling the contractor exceeds at its own risk (2420.2). The page does not rank them beyond that.

## The site's badges (of_interest.yaml) and filters

Labels are read from `of_interest.yaml` (`categories:` block, `checked: '2026-10-12'`). Each badge links to
`index.html?of_interest=<label>`; explorer.js `readUrl()` reads a multi filter from repeated `key=value` parameters, and index.html
defines `of_interest` as a multi filter over the `'; '`-split label list. All eight labels were checked against the built
contracts table on 12 Oct 2026 (contracts per label: Sole source 23, Letter contract 7, Exempt from competition 5, Rider or
cooperative purchase 2, Grew or slipped after award 2, Sole-source extension past the last option 2, Special pilot procurement 1,
Emergency procurement 1). The page does not print these counts.

Two of the yaml's plain-language `means` strings say a little more than the statute they cite; the page uses the statute's words
instead, and the owner may want to align the yaml:

- `exempt_from_competition` says "or a service a court ordered". § 2-354.13(2) says "if federal or District law prescribes with whom
  the District must contract"; it does not mention court orders.
- `special_pilot` says "try out something new on a small scale". § 2-354.08 says "an unusual or unique situation", including to
  "Obtain a new technology"; it sets no size limit.

`grew_after_award` has no `law` entry in the yaml and no single statute; the page says so and links the definitions of contract
modification and change order instead.
